U Pražské brány (German Am Prager Tor; Pragerstraße / Josefspromenade / Wilsonanlage on the plans)

U Pražské brány (“At the Prague Gate”, German Am Prager Tor; Pragerstraße, Josefspromenade and Wilsonanlage on the building plans) was a short street of old Most leading from the south-east corner of Šmeralovo náměstí (the third square) to the site of the former Prague Gate, the crossroads with Fučíkovy sady and Rybalkova. On the west side stood the corner house 250 with the funeral service and a tobacconist, the narrow butcher’s house 495 and the corner house 449 by the park; on the east side the joined corner house 247 with a portal and balcony and the three-storey corner house 249 with three gables by Rybalkova. House 251 on the south side of the square, next to 250, belongs to the batch too. We model 6 buildings; All of them are in 3D. The geometry is untextured; Codex adds textured houses one by one, and they appear in the viewer as the surface option “Textures (Codex)”.

What stood where

Positions come from the GIS layer, a vectorisation of the 1938 town plan, which lists the street with 6 buildings, all with a house number. House 251 is listed under this street but faces the square.

  • S · south side of Šmeralovo náměstí (at the street mouth): 251
  • W · west side of the street (from the square to Fučíkovy sady): 250, 495, 449
  • E · east side of the street (from the square to Rybalkova): 247, 249

Plot widths 449 and 495: GIS gives 449 only 12.0 m to the street and 10.9 m to the park, while the 1891 plan by F. Griesbach dimensions 15.00 × 17.50 m; 495 has 7.6 m in GIS, the 1930 shop-portal plan and the 1972 survey give 5.83 m. The row length in GIS is right, only the party walls are misplaced, so the model uses the plan widths and house 250 takes the remaining 25.8 m. The canonical GIS is unchanged.

Not modelled: structure 245 holds only postcards and stands in another street according to GIS. Map of the street (Czech page)

Sources and geometry

The street is documented by the KSPPOP photographic documentation of 1965–1975 (record cards naming the viewpoint and the neighbours; photographers Záša, Čermák, Poláček, Villert, Šejner), by surveys of 1972–1974 (O. Horáková: ground plans 1:50; V. Novák: facades 1:50 of houses 249 and 449), by building plans of 1891 (449), 1898–1928 (rear wing of 249) and 1930 (shop portal of 495), by hand-drawn roof sketches and by postcards. All 203 attachments of the 7 database structures were read live on 23 Oct 2026 (read-only, 0 writes) and opened, plus 550 attachments of 25 structures of the neighbouring areas. Facades are read only from photographs or surveys with the house in a red frame and every opening numbered; openings the sources do not show are declared hypotheses. A parametric Blender builder turns per-house JSON plans into models, which pass an automatic geometry gate (closed attic, UV, mesh, opening counts) and a visual review before they are placed in the street. Where a plan differs from the photograph (495: three upper windows on the 1930 plan, two on the 1974 photograph), the model follows the photograph. Heights come from the 1974 surveys and from photograph proportions; the terrain is flat for now (provisional).

Open question (249): the surveyed two-axis gabled facade is 7.65 m wide (the scan scale is calibrated on the 449 sheet), whereas GIS gives the south side 12.46 m and the photographs show three gables; the surveyed unit is placed at the corner of the south facade and its composition is reused for the gabled bay of the street facade, the rest being a hypothesis.
