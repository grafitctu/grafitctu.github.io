Pařidelská (German Paredler Straße; „Strasse Brüx–Paredl“ on the plans)

Pařidelská (German Paredler Straße; “Strasse Brüx–Paredl” on the building plans) was the road leading out of old Most towards Pařidla: from the Prague suburb by the sports ground it ran north-east along the state railway. On its south-east side, between the turn-off of Liebknechtova and the railway line, the mining company Theresia Tiefbau-Gewerkschaft built a row of double villas in the 1920s: the double villa 426 with a central risalit and gable (project by the builder Ernst Wettstein, 1922) and the villa 1425 with a pediment (1925). On the north-west side by the sports ground stood houses 421 and 1084; their database attachments could not be loaded on 29 Oct 2026 (the archive did not respond), so for now only their GIS footprints are known. Four more structures of the area (990, 1124, 1426 and 522) have no GIS footprint and are handed over as blocked. We model 4 buildings with a GIS footprint; 2 of them are in 3D so far. The geometry is untextured; Codex adds textured houses one by one, and they appear in the viewer as the surface option “Textures (Codex)”.

What stood where

Positions come from the GIS layer, a vectorisation of the 1938 town plan, which lists the street with 8 buildings: four with a house number (421 and 1084 on the north-west side, 426 and 1425 on the south-east side) and four without a number at the south-west end by the sports ground. Liebknechtova branches off to the south right behind the double villa 426; the railway ran behind the villas and the Deutscher Sportplatz lay across the road (site plan of 1922).

  • NW · north-west side (by the sports ground): 421, 1084
  • SE · south-east side (villas by Liebknechtova and the railway): 426, 1425

Footprints: both villas follow their dimensioned building plans (426: 27.00 × 12.35 m with a 0.8 m risalit, 1922; 1425: 14.00 × 9.20 m, 1925), anchored to the GIS polygons. For 426 the GIS draws a 20.3 m front projecting 2.1 m that the plan does not know; the canonical GIS is unchanged.

Not modelled: structures 990, 1124 and 1426 have no GIS footprint; structure 522 is listed live in the area Tyršova and lies under Jahnova in GIS, 730 m away (identity to verify); all their attachments were unavailable on 29 Oct 2026. Map of the street (Czech page)

Sources and geometry

Both villas have dimensioned projects by the Most builder Ernst Wettstein for the Theresia Tiefbau-Gewerkschaft: the double villa 426 of 24 July 1922 (front elevation, attic, sections a–b and c–d, plans of the cellar, ground floor and first floor, site plan) and the villa 1425 of September 1925 (elevation, section, plans, roof, site plan 1:1440) with a fence plan of 1926. The 1974 photographs with KSPPOP record cards name the viewpoints and neighbours: for 1425 the street front from across Pařidelská, the corner with Liebknechtova and the courtyard side with the entrance; for 426 the rear from Liebknechtova and the row of double villas. Older plans of 1874–1897 filed under 426 belong to an earlier house of the same number and were not used. The database was used read-only (0 writes). Structure lists come from the live read of 23 Oct 2026; on 29 Oct 2026 the archive timed out, so of 99 listed attachments only 30 could be opened (read-only copies of the Liebknechtova-area records of 426 and 1425 fetched on 23 Oct by another thread) and 69 remain unavailable, including the area’s own records and the pending houses 421 and 1084. Facades are read from photographs or plans with the house in a red frame and every opening numbered; openings the sources do not show are declared hypotheses. A parametric Blender builder turns per-house JSON plans into models, which pass an automatic geometry gate (closed attic, UV, mesh, opening counts) and a visual review before they are placed in the street. Heights come from the 1922 and 1925 elevations and sections (project, ±0.6 m); the terrain is flat for now (provisional).

Open questions: the street front of 426 is documented only by the 1922 project and a distant photograph (label “partly hypothesis”); the orientation of 1425 (pediment to the street, entrance at the rear) follows the record-card descriptions of the viewpoints; the side walls of both villas follow the plans without a photograph.
