Pařidelská: villas and houses by the Evžen mine in 3D from archival sources

Pařidelská (German Paredler Straße; „Strasse Brüx–Paredl“ on the plans)

Pařidelská (German Paredler Straße; “Strasse Brüx–Paredl” on the building plans) was the road leading out of old Most towards Pařidla: from the Prague suburb by the sports ground it ran north-east along the state railway, between the Elisa enamel works, a brickworks and the Evžen mine (later Ležáky II). On its south-east side, between the turn-off of Liebknechtova and the railway line, the mining company Theresia Tiefbau-Gewerkschaft built a row of double villas in the 1920s: the double villa 426 with a central risalit and gable (project by the builder Ernst Wettstein, 1922) and the villa 1425 with a pediment (1925). On the north-west side stood the former powder mill 421 by the sports ground, documented by a single photograph of c. 1925, and further north-east the mine building 1084, known only from distant views; both are therefore in 3D with a hypothesis label. Houses 990 and 1124 by the enamel works, house 1426 in the mine area, the brickworks 509 and house 522 of Tyršova street are not modelled. We model 4 buildings with a GIS footprint; All of them are in 3D. The geometry is untextured; Codex adds textured houses one by one, and they appear in the viewer as the surface option “Textures (Codex)”.

What stood where

Positions come from the GIS layer, a vectorisation of the 1938 town plan, which lists the street with 8 buildings: four with a house number (421 and 1084 on the north-west side, 426 and 1425 on the south-east side) and four without a number at the south-west end by the sports ground. Liebknechtova branches off to the south right behind the double villa 426; the railway ran behind the villas and the Deutscher Sportplatz lay across the road (site plan of 1922); the Evžen mine with building 1084 lay further north-east across the road.

  • SE · south-east side (villas by Liebknechtova and the railway): 426, 1425
  • NW · north-west side (by the sports ground): 421, 1084

Footprints: both villas follow their dimensioned building plans (426: 27.00 × 12.35 m with a 0.8 m risalit, 1922; 1425: 14.00 × 9.20 m, 1925), anchored to the GIS polygons; for 426 the GIS draws a 20.3 m front projecting 2.1 m that the plan does not know. Houses 421 and 1084 take their footprints directly from GIS.

Not modelled: houses 990 and 1124 by the enamel works stood until the end of Most and have demolition-era facade photographs, but GIS has no footprint for them; 1426 (demolished before 1965) has no footprint either; structure 522 belongs to Tyršova street (cards, GIS under Jahnova); structure 509 is the Hinke brickworks with plans only. Sources and geometry

Both villas have dimensioned projects by the Most builder Ernst Wettstein for the Theresia Tiefbau-Gewerkschaft: the double villa 426 of 24 July 1922 (front elevation, attic, sections a–b and c–d, plans of the cellar, ground floor and first floor, site plan) and the villa 1425 of September 1925 (elevation, section, plans, roof, site plan 1:1440) with a fence plan of 1926. The 1974 photographs with KSPPOP record cards name the viewpoints and neighbours: for 426 the street front in two views, the courtyard side in two halves and the row of double villas from Liebknechtova; for 1425 the street front from across Pařidelská, the corner with Liebknechtova and the courtyard side with the entrance. The Komplet Starý Most file adds the single photograph of the powder mill 421 (c. 1925) and two distant views of the Evžen mine (c. 1960 and 1965) for 1084 and 1426. Older plans of 1874–1897 filed under 426 (shared with structure 540) and the J. Švermy street material filed under 421 belong to other houses and were not used. The database was used read-only (0 writes). The archive did not respond on 29 Oct 2026 (recorded in the casebook); on 3 Nov 2026 all 590 attachments of 36 structures were read live and opened. Facades are read from photographs or plans with the house in a red frame and every opening numbered; openings the sources do not show are declared hypotheses. A parametric Blender builder turns per-house JSON plans into models, which pass an automatic geometry gate (closed attic, UV, mesh, opening counts) and a visual review before they are placed in the street. Heights come from the 1922 and 1925 elevations and sections (project, ±0.6 m), from photograph proportions for 421 and are estimated for 1084; the terrain is flat for now (provisional).

Open questions: when the powder mill 421 was demolished (its number was transferred to J. Švermy street after 1930, yet the 1938 plan still shows it); which of the mine buildings on the distant photographs is 1084; whether the unnumbered GIS buildings by the railway are the enamel works and houses 990/1124; the side walls of both villas follow the plans without a photograph.
