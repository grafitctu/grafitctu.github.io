Hotel Adler: what a colour postcard can tell us

Red-orange roofs and a pale yellowish façade. A postcard suggests colour families, not an exact historical shade.

A colour family, not an exact shade

The HOTEL ADLER sign above the shopfront identifies the hotel. The project associates it with Balbínova Street and building no. 83; an exact match between this reproduction and a particular archival attachment has not been verified.

The reproduction shows pale warm plaster and red-orange roof areas. The neighbouring façade on the right appears more strongly ochre. Its colour must not automatically be applied to the hotel; each roof area must be assigned to the correct building.

The printed image may have been coloured or retouched. Reproduction and ageing also affect its appearance. It does not establish exact pigments, material reflectance or calibrated RGB values.

From evidence to material choices

Roof: the red family

Red-orange areas are visible in the reproduction. Fired clay roofing is a working material interpretation, not an independently verified analysis. Roof boundaries need spatial checking.

Façade: warm cream

The postcard supports a pale cream-yellow family for the hotel. It does not justify adopting the stronger colour of its neighbour or applying the same scheme to every historical period.

Frames: weaker evidence

Dark brown wood is an existing project hypothesis. The postcard alone does not establish it: a dark opening or shadow is not unambiguous evidence of frame colour.

Evidence versus interpretation

The postcard documents colours in the reproduction. Material choices remain working hypotheses. A realistic appearance or a technical QA pass does not prove historical accuracy.

How the evidence guides colourisation

  1. Verify the identity of the hotel, neighbouring buildings, roof areas and source date.
  2. Separate colour families from unsupported exact shades. Keep material assignments consistent across views of the same building and period.
  3. Compare each result with the original: openings, lettering, damage, perspective and photographic detail must remain faithful.
  4. Retain failed variants and reasons for rejection. Treat obstruction removal and completion of hidden areas as a separate reviewed step.

This page shares one source and its interpretation. It does not create a new colourisation or modify the canonical colour database.
