What 50 means — and what it does not

There were 26 initial attempts and 24 additional edits across 26 different photographs. This does not mean 50 corrections of the same six photographs. The ratio of 8.33 attempts per accepted output describes the entire batch, not the typical number of retries for one photograph. Six of the 26 attempted sources passed: approximately 23%.

Twelve sources were skipped because multiple houses could not be assigned safely in a shared photograph. Another 23 had poor facade visibility, while one offered no useful facade geometry. Skipped photographs are not failed generations.

Accepted does not mean historically established

These three examples have no direct period colour evidence for the specific building. Cream or warm-grey plaster, red roofing and dark wood use provisional material families. Hidden surfaces and unverified shades remain unlocked hypotheses. Monochrome photographs establish visible geometry and damage, not exact hues.

What this batch teaches us

Generation is not the only cost. Identifying the house safely, retaining archive marks, preserving architecture and removing obstacles without implausible reconstruction are demanding. Houses 1915 and 699 passed automatic file checks but failed overall acceptance. Technical success is not visual or historical validation.

This example comes from the earlier combined colourization and cleanup workflow. The newer two-stage workflow separates colourization from obstacle removal and reconstruction. This single batch does not establish which model is faster and provides no separately attributable token cost.
