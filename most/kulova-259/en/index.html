Registered plaster and surface

The original photographic texture has limited effective source detail. G geometry follows the registered opening boundaries; larger bitmap dimensions alone do not restore archival information.

A plan is not automatically the built state

The 1871 plan shows four axes and two storeys; the 1874 sheet proposes a third storey. The photograph differs. The old database FRONT depicts another house. Exact frontage extent and unobserved sides remain unresolved.

Returns, frames and doors

Each opening has independently registered edges, real depth and metric UVs on returns. Local 8 mm bevels soften exposed edges. Door thresholds were inspected; steps were not copied from another house without evidence.

Roof and chimneys

Two chimneys are visible in the shared photograph. Roof silhouette informs the working envelope; the rear slope and cropped roof termination remain hypotheses.

Actual masked UV completion

Built-in image editing supplied missing materials. A protected quarter of each material strip was retained exactly. New material drawing is a hypothesis, not recovered historic detail.

A continuous texture for a large wall

The first atlas repeated conspicuously on side walls. A second actual edit supplied a calmer continuous plaster surface; deterministic colour matching preserved protected pixels. Hidden wall layout is not historically documented.

Review found flaws in the first version

Chimneys originally failed to meet the roof and sills were too bright. The comparison preserves the first and corrected versions with the same camera.

Checks and handoff

Eleven openings were inspected from the front and both oblique sides, together with four axes, roof corners, details, clay renders and source clicks. The HD alternative has its own 92-view QA; the historical geometry block remains unresolved.
