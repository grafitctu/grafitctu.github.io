Original / colourized reference

The two source images were independently rectified. Earlier colourization reconstructed the region behind a pole. Upsampling the low-resolution photograph does not add historical evidence.

Straightened shopfront — new variant

At the user’s request, horizontal shopfront construction lines and the storey cornice were straightened together with their mapping. Timber drawing and wear come from the same texture. This is a working interpretation of photo distortion, not a proven camera calibration. Before/after evidence remains available.

Plans and the correct house boundary first

Plans from 1887 and 1965 and neighbouring photographs informed the narrow working facade. A drawing labelled Hintergebäude depicts the rear building, not the street front. Exact facade identity and the combination of different epochs remain unresolved.

Window texture / real geometry

The G variant aligns openings to registered source boundaries. Returns have independent metric UVs and subtle local bevels. All openings were checked from the front and both oblique sides.

One pipe, an independent threshold and closed roof openings

The independent 3D downpipe is not duplicated in plaster. The threshold and roof-opening closures were inspected in texture and clay views. Their hidden construction and dimensions remain hypotheses.

Findings from the inspection views

Four axes, roof corners, details and opening views exposed texture and geometry issues. The requested straightening was reviewed separately from historical identity. A successful load is not historical approval.

Documentation and files

The model remains a provisional study with unresolved facade identity and unobserved walls. Download the preserved documentation, QA, GLB and editable Blender; it is not promoted into an approved street assembly.
