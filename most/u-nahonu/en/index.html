GRAFIT / MOST · U NÁHONU

U náhonu and Na popravišti: houses by the municipal slaughterhouse in 3D from archival sources

U náhonu (1938 K jatkám, German Schlachthausstraße) and the adjoining Na popravišti (German Am Rabenstein)

The short street U náhonu (on the 1938 town plan K jatkám, German Schlachthausstraße) ran in the southern part of old Most from Rybalkova along the former mill race to the municipal slaughterhouse and joined the street Na popravišti (Am Rabenstein). Its west side held the corner house 281 facing Rybalkova, the rear stables of house 286 in Leninova (rebuilt in 1928, garages in the 1970s), the pair of three-storey houses 987 and 988 by the corner of Jablonského, house 1818 with a compound roof and the ruin of house 431, which in the 1970s served as the entrance to a garage yard. The slaughterhouse grounds on the east side have only sketches and single photographs and stay grey blocks. To the south follows the south-east row of Na popravišti from the corner house 606 to the end house 1068, and inside the block the cottage 1886, known only from a sketch. Houses 455, 597, 697 and the slaughterhouse administration 838 were demolished before the available photographs were taken and are not modelled. We model 12 buildings; All of them are in 3D. The geometry is untextured; Codex adds textured houses one by one, and they appear in the viewer as the surface option “Textures (Codex)”.

What stood where

Positions come from the GIS layer, a vectorisation of the 1938 town plan, which lists U náhonu (K jatkám) with 15 buildings and Na popravišti with 11. On the west side of U náhonu lie the corners of Rybalkova (281) and B. Jablonského (988 and 1818); the slaughterhouse occupies the east side; at the south end the street joins Na popravišti, whose south-east row ends with house 1068 by an open square towards Pražská.

  • W · west side of U náhonu (from Rybalkova to Na popravišti): 281, 286, 987, 988, 1818, 431
  • NP · Na popravišti, south-east row (from the corner house 606 to the square by Pražská): 606, 1886, 719, 541, 1065, 1068

Widths: the map shows GIS footprints; the model departs from them where the sources say so: 988 and 987 are 11.40 and 12.05 m wide instead of 12.38 and 11.07 m (the GIS total of the pair is kept), 541, 1065 and 1068 take 12.80, 9.00 and 9.00 m from their dimensioned plans instead of 12.15, 10.06 and 8.44 m, the stables 286 are a 10.30 m block after the 1928 plan where GIS draws only a 4.56 m strip, and house 281 leaves out the 3.1 m corner strip, a terrace over garages according to the card. The cottage 1886 is missing in GIS and is placed after the sketch.

Not modelled: 455, 597, 697 (gaps on the 1970s photographs) and 838 (crossed out in the site sketch) are grey GIS blocks and handed over as blocked; 556, 566 and 1697 of the slaughterhouse, 550 (its main front belongs to Leninova) and the GIS building labelled 230 (the archive files a house in the Old Town under that number) stay grey blocks. Sources and geometry

The facades come from 1970s photographs: the ATO119 series (the row 988–987 from the south-east, front and corner views, courtyard sides from a neighbouring balcony) and the photographs with record cards of the regional heritage office (KSPPOP), which name viewpoints and neighbours. Dimensioned plans give widths, heights and pitches: 281 (June 1885), 719 (1885, rebuilt 1928), 1065 (October 1899), 1068 (December 1899), 431 (1922), the stables 286 (October 1928), 541 (about 1927, section 1930) and a survey of 606 (1941). A project is not the built house: the neo-baroque decoration of 1068, the rustication of 719 and the paired windows of 606 do not appear on the photographs and are not modelled. Hand-drawn KSPPOP sketches give roof layouts and the cottage 1886. The database was used read-only (0 writes). The area U náhonu was reorganised twice during the work (20 structures in the snapshot of 11 Sep 2026, 12 on 23 Oct, 7 on 9 Nov 2026), so every house number was looked up in all areas where the archive files it: all 554 attachments of 29 structures were read live and opened. Facades are read from photographs or plans with the house in a red frame and every opening numbered; openings the sources do not show are declared hypotheses. A parametric Blender builder turns per-house JSON plans into models, which pass an automatic geometry gate (closed attic, UV, mesh, opening counts) and a visual review before they are placed in the street; fixes after the review get a new revision (988 r4, 286 r5, 541 r3). The terrain is flat (provisional).

Open questions: the canonical GIS widths of 987/988 and of 541, 1065 and 1068; a variant of 431 after the 1922 plan with a roof; house 455 as a project hypothesis after its only plan; whether GIS building 1299 is part of the slaughterhouse hall 566; what stood on GIS buildings 370 and 371.
